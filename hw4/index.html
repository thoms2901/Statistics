n systems, m attackers: Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem

November 2026

m attackers each try to penetrate n systems. Every attempt gives a score Xi, and the scores are independent and identically distributed. I simulate the trajectories of the attackers and look at how the total, the average and the normalised total behave when n grows.

1. The model

For attacker j the running score after k systems is Sk = X1 + … + Xk, k = 1..n. The m attackers are m independent copies of this process, so at each n I have m values of Sn to look at.

  • Bernoulli(p): Xi = 1 if system i is penetrated, 0 otherwise. Mean μ = p, variance σ² = p(1 − p). Sn counts the penetrated systems.
  • Rademacher: Xi = +1 or −1 with probability 1/2 each. Mean 0, variance 1. This is the symmetric zero-mean case.

Since the Xi are independent, means and variances add up, and dividing by a constant c divides the variance by c²:

quantity mean variance
Sn nμ nσ²
Sn/n μ σ²/n
Sn/√n √n μ σ²

So the variance of the total grows with n, the variance of the average goes to 0, and the variance of Sn/√n does not depend on n.

4. Law of Large Numbers: Sn/n → μ

The weak LLN says that Sn/n converges to μ in probability: for every ε > 0, P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) → 0 as n → ∞. It follows from Chebyshev's inequality and the variance σ²/n of the average:

P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ² / (n ε²) → 0

I estimate the probability on the left as the fraction of attackers whose average is more than ε away from μ.

The fraction goes to 0 as n grows, which is the convergence in probability. Chebyshev's bound is always above the simulated value: it is correct but quite pessimistic, because it holds for any distribution with finite variance.

5. Central Limit Theorem: Sn/√n → N(0, 1) (Rademacher)

In the Rademacher model μ = 0 and σ = 1, so Sn/√n has mean 0 and variance 1 for every n. The CLT says that its distribution converges to the standard normal. Choosing "S_n / sqrt(n)" in section 3 shows this in the histograms. As a simple numerical check I compare a few probabilities with the normal values:

For small n the match is not perfect because Sn only takes a few integer values (for Rademacher it even moves in steps of 2), so its distribution is a set of spikes and not a smooth curve. When n grows the spikes get closer and the probabilities approach the normal ones. In the Bernoulli case Sn/√n does not converge, because its mean √n·p goes to infinity; to see the CLT there one has to centre it first, (Sn − np)/√(np(1 − p)).

6. Interpretation: n systems and m attackers

Number of systems n. The number of systems an attacker penetrates grows like nμ, with fluctuations of order σ√n. Relative to n the fluctuations disappear: with many systems the penetration rate Sn/n of an attacker is practically equal to p. In absolute terms, however, the number of penetrated systems still varies by about ±2σ√n, and this grows with n.

Number of attackers m. m does not change the behaviour of a single attacker, but it is the sample size of the simulation: with more attackers the histograms and the estimated variances are closer to the theoretical ones. The total score of all attackers together is a sum of m·n independent scores, so it has mean mnμ and variance mnσ², and the same LLN and CLT apply to it. Also, with more attackers it becomes more likely that at least one of them ends up far above the mean.

The zero-mean case. In the Rademacher model the expected score is 0 at every step, but single trajectories in Fig. 1 still go far from 0 and can stay positive or negative for a long time. Looking at one attacker only, one could think it is particularly good or bad; the average over many systems or many attackers shows that it is just chance.

7. Conclusion

Sn has a spread that grows like √n. Sn/n concentrates around μ (LLN), with a variance that goes down like 1/n. Sn/√n keeps a constant variance and in the Rademacher case its distribution becomes the standard normal (CLT). For the attack model: with many systems the penetration rate becomes predictable, while the absolute number of penetrated systems keeps fluctuating.
